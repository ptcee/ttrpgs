A slice of life TTRPG using one deck of cards.

For 2-4 players  ·  Brightmist Wood  ·  Orrelia

Within the Brightmist Wood, a young kobold and their friends slip away from camp while their parents set up tents and cook dinner. What follows is a quiet afternoon of wandering, pretending, and discovery; unremarkable events to grown-ups, but grand and enchanting to youngsters.

You are those youngsters, and this is how you spend the afternoon.

Kobold Campout is meant to evoke the likes of a comic strip; Calvin and Hobbes walking through the woods talking to each other, dictated and led by your hand of playing cards.

You Need

  • 1 standard card deck (jokers removed)
  • 1 character card per player
  • Pencils
  • The rules

Characters

You play as a young elf, dwarf, goblin, and kobold walking through the forest northeast of Glimmerhold, on a camping trip with the kobold's family. The parents are back at the campsite setting up and making dinner.

At the beginning of the game, remove the aces from the deck, shuffle them, and deal one to each player.

Questions

Before play begins, each player asks one question to the player on their left and one to the player on their right. Write down the answers on your character sheet.

Treat answers as true for the rest of the game, even if they change how you imagine your character later.

♥ Elf

  • ← Left What did you see that you weren't supposed to?
  • → Right What do you think is the prettiest thing out here?

♦ Dwarf

  • ← Left What did you bring that nobody else thought to bring?
  • → Right What are you going to tell everyone when we get back?

♠ Goblin

  • ← Left What's the best thing that's happened so far?
  • → Right What should we definitely do even if it's a bad idea?

♣ Kobold

  • ← Left What did you find that you wish you could keep forever?
  • → Right What makes you feel safe when the woods get too quiet?

Then write your Truth on your character card. This is one sentence about who your character is at their core. Think about your kid's personality and who they are.

Once questions are answered and your Truth is written, shuffle all cards back into the deck.

To Start

Deal 3 cards to each player.

Place the top 3 cards of the deck in the centre of the table. These are the locations where your adventure takes place. Reveal the first one.

Locations

The three locations form the shape of your afternoon:

  1. Somewhere you find - a discovery, something that catches your eye.
  2. Somewhere something happens - the heart of the adventure.
  3. Somewhere you head back from - the walk back toward camp and dinner.

These are not geographic labels, but emotional phases of the afternoon.

Playing the Game

Players take turns playing a card from their hand, using it to dictate the direction of the conversation - describing what their character does, says, or notices as they explore.

When all cards have been played the round is over. Update your Truths, deal new hands of 3, then reveal the next location card. The player whose suit matches the location starts; play continues clockwise.

After all three locations, the kids find their way back to camp.

Creating the Fiction

On your turn, play a card and describe what your character does, says, or feels based on it. Any player may respond in the moment without playing a card, but only the active player advances the story. If you respond, keep it to one or two sentences, then focus returns to the active player. If a card involves your suit, you may always respond first.

When you play a card, interpret colour, value, and suit together in whatever order best fits the moment.

Remember - even the smallest card can describe something incredible through the lens of a kid.

Changing Your Truth

At the end of each location, check if anyone played your suit. If yes, adjust your Truth by adding, removing, or changing exactly one word based on what happened. Write the new version underneath your old Truth.

At the end of the game, the Kings on the table form a trail of the afternoon's most memorable moments - a little timeline of what happened out in the woods. Kings always escalate or advance the scene before play continues.

Card Suit

The suit tells you who the card involves. If the suit matches your character, the card is about you. Otherwise, it is about your relationship with that character.

Ending the Game

Once the group has returned to camp, the table answers three questions together:

  • What did I learn about my friends?
  • Who was the most surprising, and why?
  • How am I different than when we started? (Read your first Truth, then read your last Truth.)

Afterwards everyone curls up in their bedrolls and tucks away for the night.
